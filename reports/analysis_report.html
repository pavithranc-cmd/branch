Employee Data Analysis – Part 2: Visualisations, Report and Presentation¶

Python & Data Engineering · Week 1 · Day 5 · Weekly Project

1. Project Introduction¶

Part 1 (week1/day5/task1_data_cleaning and task2_exploratory_analysis) cleaned the employee dataset and explored it with tables. Part 2 turns that cleaned data into five charts, a one-page summary report, an HTML export of this notebook, a Streamlit dashboard and a five-minute presentation.

All chart code lives in one shared module, src/employee_report.py. This notebook, the saved PNG files and the Streamlit app all call the same functions, so they always show the same numbers.

2. Problem Statement¶

Management wants to understand how salaries are distributed across departments and cities:

  • Which departments pay the most and the least?
  • Is pay spread evenly, or concentrated in a few people?
  • Are there unusual (outlier) salaries?
  • What should be investigated or changed next?

3. Dataset Overview¶

Changes to the requested charts (the columns do not exist):

  • Experience vs salary scatter → Salary by city scatter: each employee is a point, labelled with name and department.
  • Hiring trend over time line chart → Payroll concentration curve: a line showing what share of total payroll goes to the lowest-paid X % of staff.

No experience values or dates were invented. Both replacements use real columns only.

Chart helper¶

Each visualization below calls show_and_save(): it draws the chart with the shared module, saves a 200-dpi PNG to reports/figures/, displays it, and prints the insight sentence (computed from the data, not typed by hand).

saved_files = []

def show_and_save(chart_function, filename, insight):
    fig = chart_function(df)
    path = er.save_figure(fig, filename)
    saved_files.append(path)
    plt.show()
    print(f"Saved: {path.relative_to(ROOT)}")
    print(f"Insight: {insight}")

  1. Department is the clearest driver of pay. Averages go from 81,000 (Finance, 1 person) and 67,000 (IT) down to 58,500 (Sales) and 46,500 (HR). HR is 15,000 (24 %) below the company average of 61,500, and both HR employees are among the three lowest salaries.
  2. Pay is fairly evenly spread, with no outliers. Mean = median = 61,500, the lowest-paid half of staff receive 41.3 % of payroll, the Gini coefficient is 0.11, and 0 salaries fall outside the IQR fences (22,875–97,875).
  3. The city gap probably reflects department mix. Chennai and Hyderabad average 68,000 and Bangalore 48,500. However, Bangalore's two employees work in HR and Sales, the two lowest-paid departments, so with 8 rows we cannot tell location and department apart.

13. Recommendations¶

  1. Review HR salaries against the market. HR averages 46,500, 24 % below the company average. Both HR staff are in the bottom three salaries. Benchmark these roles before it becomes a retention risk.
  2. Collect the missing fields before making pay decisions. Add joining date, years of experience, job level and salary currency/period, and grow the dataset beyond 8 people. Then the two charts that had to be replaced (experience vs salary, hiring trend) can be built, and we can test whether the department and city gaps are explained by seniority.

14. Limitations¶

  • Only 8 employees. One salary changes a group average a lot. Finance's "highest average" is a single person.
  • No experience or date columns, so the requested experience scatter and hiring-trend line were replaced with honest alternatives.
  • Only one numeric measure (salary), so no meaningful correlation analysis is possible.
  • Salary currency and pay period are not stated, so the charts label salary as "currency not stated".
  • The findings describe this sample only. They are not statistically significant.

15. Conclusion¶

The cleaned Part 1 dataset was turned into five labelled, high-resolution charts. They show that department is the main visible driver of salary: HR is clearly the lowest-paid group. Overall pay is evenly spread, with no outliers. The biggest next step is better data (experience, dates, more employees), so that pay differences can be explained rather than only described.
